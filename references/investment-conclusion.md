# Final Conclusion and Scenario Decision

Use this reference for Part 4 of a complete report and whenever the user asks whether an analyzed company is attractive to buy, hold, watch, avoid, or reduce.

## Required synthesis

Keep the three analytical layers separate before combining them:

1. **Fund-flow conclusion:** broad participation, concentrated leadership, de-risking, short covering, or inconclusive.
2. **Industry-development conclusion:** early adoption, accelerating adoption, mainstream expansion, maturing, consolidating, or structurally challenged. State the demand, competition, and business-model evidence.
3. **Company conclusion:** business quality, growth quality, moat evidence, execution, valuation, and risk.

Do not average these layers into an unexplained score. Explain conflicts explicitly. A strong company can remain unattractive at the current price; weak flows can coexist with improving fundamentals.

## Scenario table

For each company, show:

| Scenario | Operating assumptions | Valuation method | Implied value or return | Catalysts | Falsifiers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | Revenue, retention, margin, cash flow, dilution, and competitive assumptions | Multiple or DCF inputs | Downside from the dated current price | What could lead here | Evidence that breaks the base thesis |
| Base | Most defensible assumptions supported by current evidence | Same method or reconciled method | Expected return over the stated horizon | Required execution | Evidence that invalidates the base case |
| Bull | Upside assumptions that require additional evidence | Same method or reconciled method | Upside from the dated current price | Upside catalysts | Evidence that makes the bull case unlikely |

Use one currency, a common valuation date, and a stated horizon. Do not assign scenario probabilities without explaining their basis. If market price, enterprise value inputs, consensus, or a defensible valuation method are missing, provide a fundamental stance and label the action **No Rating**.

## Conditional action labels

Fix the decision thresholds before comparing the scenario outputs. Use user-supplied thresholds when available. Otherwise disclose these default research conventions:

- **Buy / Accumulate:** base-case expected return is at least 20% over the stated horizon, bear-case downside is no worse than 25%, the thesis has identifiable catalysts, and no critical falsifier is already present.
- **Watchlist / Hold:** base-case expected return is between 0% and 20%, valuation is near fair value, or evidence is promising but incomplete.
- **Avoid / Reduce:** base-case expected return is below 0%, the current price requires the bull case, or a material thesis falsifier is present.
- **Speculative / High Risk:** base-case upside meets the buy hurdle but bear-case downside exceeds 25%, scenario dispersion is unusually wide, or the thesis depends on evidence not yet established.
- **No Rating:** current price, valuation inputs, comparable disclosures, or scenario evidence are insufficient.

These are reproducible research labels, not universal rules or personalized portfolio advice. Always show the threshold used. Do not convert a fundamental conclusion into a buy label when valuation data are missing.

## Required final block

End Part 4 with:

- fund-flow conclusion;
- industry-development position;
- one action label for each analyzed company;
- current price and valuation date;
- base-case expected return and bear-case downside;
- entry or accumulation condition;
- thesis-breaking condition;
- next catalyst and next monitoring date or event;
- the main data limitation.

For multiple companies, apply the same predeclared thresholds to each. Rank them only when the peer universe and ranking criteria were fixed before observing the results.
